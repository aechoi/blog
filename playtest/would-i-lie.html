Overview

Three teams take turns telling bizarre stories about themselves. Some are true, some are someone else’s truth being passed off as a lie. Fool the other teams, catch their lies, and write truths so strange nobody believes them.

  • Players: 3 teams of any size (2–3 per team works best)
  • You need: index cards, pens, a phone timer
  • Time: about 45–90 minutes
  • Goal: have the most points when the game ends

Setup

1. Write cards. Each player secretly writes true facts about themselves, one per card. Each team should end up with the same even number of cards (8–12 is a good target).

  • It must be true.
  • The stranger the better. Truths that sound like lies score well.
  • No names, places, or details that point to a specific person.
  • Nothing the other players already know about you.
  • Write your initials somewhere hidden (inside a fold or a back corner) so your team can tell whose card it is.

2. Sit in a triangle. Teams sit as three groups around the table.

3. Build the piles. Each team shuffles its cards and splits them into two equal halves. Put one half face down between you and the team on your left, and the other half between you and the team on your right. Shuffle each of the three resulting piles. Each pile now mixes the cards of the two teams beside it.

On your turn

Your team plays two rounds: first with the pile on your left, then the pile on your right. In each round, the guessers are the team sitting across from that pile. They wrote none of its cards, so they can’t recognize anything.

  1. Draw. Take the top card from the pile without showing it.
  2. Huddle (30 s). Your team quietly reads the card.
    • Your team’s card: its author reads it. It’s a truth.
    • The other team’s card: pick anyone on your team to read it. It’s a lie.
    • Always use the full 30 seconds, even if you know immediately. Leaving early is a tell.
  3. Statement. The reader reads the card aloud as their own story.
  4. Interrogation (90 s). The timer starts once the statement is finished. Guessers ask anything they like.
    • Truth: you must answer every question honestly.
    • Lie: make up whatever you need.
  5. Verdict (30 s). Guessers confer, then announce “Truth” or “Lie.”
  6. Reveal and score. The reader reveals the answer. Score it using the table below.

After both rounds, the turn passes clockwise.

Scoring

Guessers score 1 point for every correct call. A successful lie is worth 2 points to the liar, and a well-written card always earns its author something.

The card was… Guessers say “Truth” Guessers say “Lie”
True Guessers +1 Reader’s team +1
A lie Liar’s team +2 Guessers +1, card author’s team +1

All points go to teams. Keep a running tally on a spare card.

Ending the game

Stop at the end of a full rotation once about half of each pile has been played, so every team has had the same number of turns. Never play a pile down to its last cards, because a nearly empty pile becomes predictable. The team with the most points wins. Leftover cards can start the next game.

Table manners

  • Bystanders keep time. The team that is neither reading nor guessing runs the timer for the huddle, interrogation, and verdict.
  • Bystanders stay neutral. The third team can always tell whether a card is true, because they know whether it’s theirs. No reactions, no hints, no faces.
  • Already heard it? If a guesser recognizes the story, they say so right away. Discard the card and draw again.
  • Truth-tellers can’t dodge. Answering “I don’t remember” to everything counts as evasion. Give real answers.
  • Liars commit. Once you’re reading someone else’s truth, it’s yours. Sell it.

Quick reference

Draw → Huddle 30 s → Read → Interrogate 90 s → Verdict 30 s → Reveal

Use the left pile first, then the right pile, then pass the turn clockwise.

Your card means it’s a truth, read by its author. Their card means it’s a lie, read by anyone.

Scoring: a correct guess is +1 for the guessers. A truth called a lie is +1 for the reader. A believed lie is +2 for the liar. A caught lie is +1 for the guessers and +1 for the card’s author.
